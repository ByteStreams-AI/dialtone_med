# Inbound phone-call volume and call-center performance at US specialty pharmacies (as of November 2026)

Evidence labels used throughout: **[ACCREDITOR]** accreditor-reported (audited, aggregate), **[PEER-REVIEWED]** measured or surveyed in a journal article, **[SURVEY]** industry survey, **[ANALYST]** Drug Channels Institute analysis, **[VENDOR]** vendor marketing or case study, **[DERIVED]** my arithmetic on cited figures.

Headline: no source publishes inbound calls per specialty pharmacy location. The best available basis is URAC's audited aggregate totals, which give the total calls and total specialty prescriptions for the same set of reporting organizations. That yields about 0.9 to 1.3 inbound business-hours calls per specialty prescription dispensed (three-year average 1.1).

## 1. What do URAC and ACHC require and measure for call centers, and what are the latest aggregate results? Are 2.76% abandonment and 84% answered within 30 seconds correct?

### Takeaway
The two figures are confirmed, with one precision: they come from URAC's 2024 report, which covers the 2023 measurement year (2.76% abandonment; 84.48% answered by a live voice within 30 seconds). A newer report exists (2025 report, 2024 measurement year, published January 2026): 2.98% abandonment and 83.15% within 30 seconds across 446 to 451 submissions. URAC's thresholds are 5% or less abandonment and 80% of calls within 30 seconds; ACHC requires call-abandonment and response-time tracking logs but I found no numeric ACHC threshold and no ACHC aggregate results.

### Cited Findings

**URAC measure definition [ACCREDITOR]**
- The measure is Call Center Performance (DTM2010-04), mandatory, two parts. Part A: percentage of calls during normal business hours to the organization's call service center(s) answered by a live voice within 30 seconds. Part B: percentage of calls during normal business hours abandoned by callers before being answered by a live customer service representative. No stratification; reported across all populations. — [URAC 2025 Specialty Pharmacy Aggregate Summary Performance Report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25)
- Mandatory measures are subject to external data validation by data validation vendors (audited). — [URAC 2025 report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25)
- URAC's own readiness checklist states the thresholds: "Average Abandonment Rate (5% or less) and Average Speed of Answer (80% of calls within 30 seconds)", to be checked in monthly and quarterly telephone metric reports. It also requires clinical inquiries to be handled 24/7. PDF last modified September 2022 (Specialty Pharmacy 5.0 era). — [URAC "Are you ready for URAC's Specialty Pharmacy Accreditation?"](https://www.urac.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Are-You-Readdy-URAC-SPP-5.0.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "2.76% / 84%" pair should be cited as "URAC 2024 report, 2023 measurement year, 393 to 396 submissions". If the deck or site wants the most current figure, use 2.98% and 83.15% (URAC 2025 report, 2024 measurement year, published January 2026). The aggregate 30-second rate has declined three years running (86.24% to 84.48% to 83.15%) while abandonment drifted up (2.73% to 2.76% to 2.98%).
- Because the 10th-percentile abandonment rate is 5.89%, more than 10% of URAC-accredited specialty pharmacies exceed URAC's 5% abandonment threshold. Because the 10th-percentile 30-second rate is 74.96% and the 25th is 84.32%, somewhere between 10% and 25% of organizations miss the 80%-in-30-seconds threshold. These are the organizations with a compliance reason to buy call-answering capacity.
- About 1.59 million calls to URAC-accredited specialty pharmacies were abandoned in 2024 (Part B numerator).
- The URAC measure counts live-voice answers only. Whether a call fully resolved by an automated or AI agent would count as "answered by a live voice" is not addressed in the reports I read; this matters for positioning and should be checked against the measure specification before any accreditation-benefit claim is made.

### Gaps
- Blockage rate and average speed of answer in seconds: neither appears in URAC's specialty pharmacy aggregate reports for 2023 to 2025. The specialty pharmacy measure has only the two parts above. I did not find a URAC or ACHC specialty pharmacy blockage-rate requirement.
- ACHC numeric thresholds: not found. The only ACHC evidence is the survey-items list requiring tracking logs. ACHC publishes no aggregate call-performance results that I could find.
- I could not open the full URAC measure specification (the "Measures at a Glance" PDF failed to download), so details such as whether voicemail or callback counts as abandoned, IVR handling, and whether prescriber calls are included are unconfirmed for current editions. A search snippet attributed to an earlier URAC report says calls are still counted as abandoned if the caller leaves a voicemail or accepts a callback; I did not locate that text in the 2023 to 2025 reports.
- A search-result summary of the 2023 URAC snapshot suggested "97% answered within 30 seconds"; that is a text-extraction artifact. The report itself says 86.24%.

## 2. Does any body publish inbound call volume per pharmacy, per patient, per prescription or per FTE?

### Takeaway
No source publishes inbound calls per pharmacy location, per patient or per FTE. URAC is the only source with real volume data: its audited totals show roughly 53 million business-hours calls a year against 44 to 58 million specialty prescriptions, which gives 0.91 to 1.27 calls per specialty prescription (derived, not published as a ratio by URAC).

### Cited Findings
- Total calls to call service centers (Part B denominators) [ACCREDITOR]: 55,391,526 (MY 2022, 384 submissions); 53,004,703 (MY 2023, 396 submissions); 53,383,561 (MY 2024, 451 submissions). — [URAC 2023 report](https://www.urac.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/URAC__Specialty-Pharmacy_Aggregate-Summary-Report_2023_FINAL.pdf); [URAC 2024 report](https://2297879.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/2297879/URAC_Specialty%20Pharmacy_Aggregate%20Summary%20Report_2024.pdf); [URAC 2025 report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25)
- Total specialty prescriptions dispensed by all reporting organizations [ACCREDITOR]: 43,772,751 (MY 2022, 400 orgs); 46,897,711 (MY 2023, 422 orgs); 58,396,793 (MY 2024, 465 orgs). — same three reports
- Not all reporting organizations dispense mainly specialty drugs: only 198 of 465 (about 43%) reported dispensing at least 99% specialty drugs in MY 2024 (145 of 422 in MY 2023). — [URAC 2025 report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25); [URAC 2024 report](https://2297879.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/2297879/URAC_Specialty%20Pharmacy_Aggregate%20Summary%20Report_2024.pdf)
- Volume is extremely concentrated: specialty prescriptions per organization ranged from 79 to 15,279,673 in MY 2024; organizations identifying as Large Chain were about 3% of reporters but more than 35% of dispensing volume. — [URAC 2025 report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25)
- [VENDOR] A patient-support company operating non-commercial dispensing pharmacies (patient assistance, quick-start and bridge programs) states "our pharmacies receive approximately 12,000 calls per month", with 60 to 70% "routine and repeatable"; number of pharmacies and patients not stated; published 13 January 2026. — [Valeris blog](https://www.valeris.com/resources/building-the-use-case-the-potential-for-ai-assistants-to-shorten-pharmacy-call-times/)
- [VENDOR, not specialty-specific] A call-center outsourcer's case study of a large West Coast health system reports prescription-refill calls alone reaching 22,000 per month, with abandonment falling from 11% to 1.89% after outsourcing. This is a health-system refill line, not a specialty pharmacy. — [Carenet Health case study](https://carenethealthcare.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Carenet-Health-A-Large-West-Coast-Health-System-Case-Study-.pdf) (search-result summary; PDF not read in full)

### Inferences
- [DERIVED] Calls per specialty prescription = Part B call denominator / total specialty prescriptions:
  - MY 2022: 55,391,526 / 43,772,751 = 1.27
  - MY 2023: 53,004,703 / 46,897,711 = 1.13
  - MY 2024: 53,383,561 / 58,396,793 = 0.91
  - Three-year simple average = 1.10
- [DERIVED] Mean calls per reporting organization: 53,383,561 / 451 = about 118,400 per year, about 9,900 per month (MY 2024). This mean is dominated by a handful of national organizations and is not typical; see question 4 for the size distribution.
- Caveats on the ratio (confidence: medium):
  1. Numerator and denominator come from slightly different organization sets (451 call submissions versus 465 organizations reporting prescriptions).
  2. The call count is every business-hours call to the call center, so for the 57% of organizations that also dispense non-specialty drugs it includes non-specialty calls. This biases calls-per-specialty-prescription upward.
  3. After-hours calls are excluded, which biases total inbound volume downward.
  4. It is volume-weighted, so it mostly describes the largest PBM-owned and chain organizations. Small independents and health-system pharmacies may differ in either direction; there is no stratified data.
  5. The ratio fell in MY 2024 because prescription volume rose 24% while calls were flat. Whether that reflects digital deflection or a change in the mix of reporters is unknown.
- The URAC totals also give a floor on the addressable call pool: about 53 million business-hours calls per year (about 4.4 million per month) at URAC-accredited specialty pharmacy organizations alone, excluding ACHC-only pharmacies and after-hours calls.

### Gaps
- No inbound calls per location, per patient or per pharmacist/technician FTE in URAC, ACHC, Drug Channels, the ASHP health-system specialty pharmacy surveys, or the vendor material I found. State this plainly in the report.
- NASP: I found no NASP publication with call volume. I did not exhaust NASP member-only material.
- JMCP, AJHP, JAPhA: no article reporting inbound call counts for a specialty pharmacy was found. The ASHP 2022 workforce survey (Kelley et al., AJHP 2023, [PubMed 37742303](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37742303/)) could not be read (page blocked); it may contain staffing figures.

## 3. Inbound versus outbound calls per patient per month; time-and-motion and workload studies

### Takeaway
No study quantifies inbound or outbound calls per patient per month. The literature shows the phone is the dominant channel (all health-system specialty pharmacies use it for monitoring; texting is rare), refill outreach protocols run to three call attempts per fill, and each refill call costs about four minutes of documentation alone.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER-REVIEWED, survey] ASHP 2024 survey of health-system specialty pharmacies (71 respondents of 298 contacted, 23.8% response): telephone is the most common channel for monitoring, used by all respondents and "frequently or always" by 93%; used frequently or always for initial medication counseling by 80%; SMS messaging rarely used by 76%; refill reminders provided by 92%. Technology in use or planned within 6 to 12 months: electronic refill requests 69%, one-way texting 47%, two-way texting 47%, patient portal 80%, online chat 16%. — [ASHP Survey of Health-System Specialty Pharmacy Practice: Clinical Services 2024 (AJHP, via PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13747721)
- [PEER-REVIEWED, measured] Vanderbilt Specialty Pharmacy (health-system-integrated; "fills more than 5,000 prescriptions per month"; 6,166 patients with three or more specialty fills across 18 clinics, March 2017 to February 2018): refill outreach protocol is "at least 3 phone calls followed by a letter"; of 61 truly nonadherent patients reviewed, 36 (59.0%) were unreachable. — [JMCP, via PMC10398139](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10398139)
- [PEER-REVIEWED, measured] A 2024 pre/post direct-observation time study at a health-system specialty pharmacy (one technician per clinic, five clinics, about 10 refill calls per clinician) found refill-call documentation took about 4.1 to 4.2 minutes per refill. — [PMC11231745](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231745) (search-result summary; full text not read)
- [SURVEY, vendor-sponsored] Nearly two-thirds of specialty pharmacists report spending more than 15 minutes on the phone to fill one prescription; one in three spends at least half an hour. — [Pharmacy Times on Surescripts survey](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/survey-shows-time-to-fill-delays-of-up-to-10-days-for-specialty-prescriptions); [Surescripts data brief](https://engage.surescripts.com/themes/surescripts/templates/landing-pages/assets/pdf/surescripts-specialty-pharmacy-data-brief.pdf) (search-result summaries)
- [PEER-REVIEWED, review] Time per prior authorization in health-system pharmacies ranged from 15 to 24 minutes across four studies. — [AJMC, Prescription Drug Prior Authorization: Costs to Pharmacies and Physicians (2026)](https://ajmc.com/view/prescription-drug-prior-authorization-costs-to-pharmacies-and-physicians) (search-result summary)
- [VENDOR] A health-system specialty pharmacy moved refill and delivery coordination to secure messaging for more than 3,500 patients (about 58% of all pharmacy patients, about 100 new per month, 2021 data): delivery coordination time fell 57% (about 70 hours to about 30 hours); staff reported "a considerable reduction in phone call volume" with no count given. — [Health Catalyst case study](https://www.healthcatalyst.com/success_stories/ensuring-convenient-refill-and-delivery-of-specialty-medications)
- [Resident project, not peer-reviewed] At a Providence health-system specialty pharmacy (multiple sclerosis patients, 2019 to 2020), two-way texting cut refill-outreach response time from 68:34 to 32:03 hours; phone call volume was a secondary outcome but its result was not in the abstract. — [Providence digital commons](https://digitalcommons.providence.org/pharmacy_PGY1/12) (search-result summary)

### Inferences
- Outbound volume is probably at least as large as inbound. Accredited pharmacies make a refill-coordination contact before each fill, and protocols such as Vanderbilt's allow three attempts, so outbound attempts are plausibly 1 to 3 per fill, about 0.8 to 2.5 per patient per month at roughly 0.8 fills per patient per month. This is an inference from protocol descriptions, not a measured figure. Confidence: low.
- A share of inbound calls is callback traffic generated by missed outbound refill calls (59% of nonadherent patients were unreachable by phone at Vanderbilt). An agent that both places refill outreach and answers the returned calls addresses both sides.
- Texting adoption is low (76% of health-system specialty pharmacies rarely use SMS; under half have or plan texting), so the text channel is under-served in this segment.

### Gaps
- No measured inbound or outbound calls per patient per month for any US specialty pharmacy.
- No time-and-motion study splitting pharmacist versus technician phone minutes, or isolating benefits investigation, copay assistance or delivery-scheduling calls.
- One vendor version of the messaging case study reportedly cites a 31% reduction in inbound calls; the page I fetched did not contain that number, so treat it as unverified.

## 4. Size distribution of specialty pharmacies and number of accredited locations

### Takeaway
Drug Channels Institute's count of 1,910 accredited locations at end-2025 is confirmed (ACHC and/or URAC; up 3% from 1,861). Independents and hospital/health-system pharmacies are each roughly 28 to 29%, other providers 16%, with the remaining roughly 27% (PBM/health plan, retail chain, wholesaler, other) shown only in a chart image I could not read. Most accredited organizations are small: 56% of URAC reporters dispense under 16,000 specialty prescriptions a year, while fewer than 1% dispense 2 million or more.

### Cited Findings
**Accredited location count and ownership [ANALYST]**
- "1,910 unique pharmacy locations that had achieved specialty pharmacy accreditation from ACHC and/or URAC" at end of 2025; "a 3% increase over the 1,861 locations in 2024"; more than five times the 2015 figure. — [Drug Channels, 5 May 2026](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/05/specialty-pharmacy-accreditation-dcis.html)
- 677 locations hold both ACHC and URAC accreditation (up 85 from 2024); 1,233 hold only one (down 36). — [Drug Channels, 5 May 2026](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/05/specialty-pharmacy-accreditation-dcis.html)
- Independents (private, private-equity-owned, independent franchise): 553 locations, 29%, down from 59% in 2015. — [Drug Channels, 5 May 2026](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/05/specialty-pharmacy-accreditation-dcis.html)
- Hospitals and health systems: 28% of accredited locations in 2025, up from 15% in 2017; locations grew "from 106 locations in 2017 to 553 locations in 2025". — [Drug Channels, 5 May 2026](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/05/specialty-pharmacy-accreditation-dcis.html); [Drug Channels, Top 15 Specialty Pharmacies of 2025](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/04/the-top-15-specialty-pharmacies-of-2025.html)
- Other healthcare providers (physician practices, clinics, non-physician providers): 16%. — [Drug Channels, 5 May 2026](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/05/specialty-pharmacy-accreditation-dcis.html)
- Hospitals were 27% in 2024 and "nearly one-quarter of the 1,651 total accredited specialty pharmacy locations" at end-2022. — [Drug Channels, Nov 2023](https://www.drugchannels.net/2023/11/vertical-integration-lessons-economics.html)
- Revenue is concentrated in a way location counts hide: the three largest specialty pharmacies, all owned by organizations that also own a PBM, took two-thirds of pharmacy-dispensed specialty drug revenue in 2025; total specialty dispensing revenue was an estimated $293.4 billion, up 9.6%. Independents are the largest category by locations but a minority of revenue. — [Drug Channels, Top 15 Specialty Pharmacies of 2025](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/04/the-top-15-specialty-pharmacies-of-2025.html)

**Size distribution, all URAC-accredited organizations [ACCREDITOR]**
- MY 2024 (n=465 organizations), specialty prescriptions dispensed per year: under 16,000: 56.13%; 16,000 to 24,999: 9.25%; 25,000 to 99,999: 23.66%; 100,000 to 249,999: 6.67%; 250,000 to 1.99 million: 3.44%; 2 million or more: 0.86%. — [URAC 2025 report, Figure 1](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25)
- MY 2023 (n=422): 56.16%; 11.14%; 24.17%; 4.74%; 3.08%; 0.71% for the same tiers. — [URAC 2024 report, Figure 1](https://2297879.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/2297879/URAC_Specialty%20Pharmacy_Aggregate%20Summary%20Report_2024.pdf)
- Most reporters self-identify as university/hospital health system or independent; Large Chain is about 3% of organizations and more than 35% of volume. — [URAC 2025 report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25)

**Size distribution, health-system specialty pharmacies [PEER-REVIEWED, survey]**
- ASHP 2024 survey (n=64 answering), annual specialty prescriptions filled: under 7,500: 11%; 7,501 to 15,000: 13%; 15,001 to 30,000: 20%; 30,001 to 45,000: 14%; 45,001 to 60,000: 9%; 60,001 to 75,000: 20%; over 75,000: 13%. 44% under 30,000; 33% over 60,000. 67% are affiliated with an academic medical center. — [ASHP 2024 survey, via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13747721)
- ASHP 2022 survey (n=116): under 15,000: 40%; 15,001 to 30,000: 21%; 30,001 to 45,000: 15%; over 45,000: 25%. — [ASHP 2024 survey, comparison table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13747721)
- One-quarter of health-system specialty pharmacies had annual revenue above $100 million in 2022; about 98% participate in 340B. — [Drug Channels, Nov 2023, citing ASHP 2022](https://www.drugchannels.net/2023/11/vertical-integration-lessons-economics.html)
- Single-site reference point: Vanderbilt Specialty Pharmacy filled more than 5,000 prescriptions per month for 6,166 patients (three or more fills) in 2017 to 2018. — [PMC10398139](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10398139)

### Inferences
- The Drug Channels text gives 553 as the count for both independents (29%) and hospitals/health systems (28%). 553 / 1,910 = 28.95%, which rounds to 29%, so either the two categories coincidentally tie and the 28% is a rounding inconsistency, or one count is misattributed. Use "roughly 29% each, about 550 locations each" and flag the ambiguity. The remaining share (100% minus 29% minus 28 or 29% minus 16% = about 26 to 27%, about 500 locations) is PBM/health plan, retail chain, wholesaler and other combined; the split is not in the article text.
- URAC counts organizations (465) while Drug Channels counts locations (1,910 across both accreditors). A multi-site organization reports once to URAC, so per-location volumes are equal to or lower than the per-organization figures.
- [DERIVED] Fills per patient: Vanderbilt's figures imply roughly 60,000 or more prescriptions a year over 6,166 patients, about 9.7 fills per patient-year, about 0.8 per patient-month. This is rough (the patient count excludes those with fewer than three fills, and "more than 5,000" is a floor). It is consistent with 28 to 30-day specialty dispensing.
- The target segment for a per-location product is the roughly 1,400 non-PBM, non-chain locations (independent about 550, health system about 550, other provider about 300).

### Gaps
- Exact end-2025 shares for PBM/health plan, retail chain and wholesaler-owned locations: in a Drug Channels chart image, not retrievable as text.
- Patients served and staff (pharmacist/technician FTE) per specialty pharmacy by ownership type: no source found. The ASHP 2024 clinical-services paper does not report FTEs, patients served, revenue or locations; the 2022 workforce paper was not accessible.
- No size distribution exists specifically for independent specialty pharmacies; the URAC tiers pool all types.

## 5. What share of specialty pharmacy calls concern refills, prior authorization, billing/copay, insurance, delivery, clinical questions?

### Takeaway
I found no reliable published breakdown of specialty pharmacy inbound calls by reason. The only quantified statements are vendor estimates that 60 to 85% of calls are routine status and refill-type inquiries.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] "An estimated 60-70% of those calls are what we consider 'routine and repeatable'"; a separate internal prediction in the same post says 85% of inbound calls are low-complexity (the post does not reconcile the two). Call types listed, without percentages: prescription verification, medication availability, status updates (refill requests, order status, shipment scheduling, address changes), basic administration questions, system navigation, compliance/documentation. Applies to non-commercial dispensing pharmacies; prior authorization and billing are not mentioned. — [Valeris blog, Jan 2026](https://www.valeris.com/resources/building-the-use-case-the-potential-for-ai-assistants-to-shorten-pharmacy-call-times/)
- [Trade article, not specialty-specific] For pharmacy callbacks to prescriber offices, prior authorizations (37%), formulary issues (26%) and lack of clarity about refill status (11%) were cited as the leading prescription issues. This describes pharmacy-to-practice callbacks, not patient calls to a specialty pharmacy. — [Ophthalmology Management, Nov 2019](https://ophthalmologymanagement.com/issues/2019/november/reducing-pharmacy-callbacks) (search-result summary; original data source not verified)
- [Patient-facing material] Health-system specialty pharmacy FAQs name delivery delays or mistakes, prior-authorization-driven ship-date slips and refill coordination as the reasons patients call, without shares. — [Emory Healthcare specialty pharmacy FAQ](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/services/pharmacy/specialty/faq); [Yale New Haven Health outpatient pharmacy services](https://www.ynhhs.org/patient-care/Outpatient-Pharmacy-Services) (search-result summaries)

### Inferences
- The qualitative picture is consistent: refill scheduling, order/shipment status and delivery logistics dominate patient calls; prior authorization and benefits status dominate prescriber-office calls. No percentages can be responsibly attached to the five product use cases (appointments, billing and payment, refills, prior authorization status, insurance verification).
- "Appointments" is not a natural specialty pharmacy call category; the analogue is delivery or pickup scheduling. Site copy for this segment should say delivery scheduling.

### Gaps
- No accreditor, peer-reviewed or survey source gives call-reason shares for specialty pharmacies. URAC does not collect call disposition.
- The 60 to 85% "routine" figure is a single vendor's estimate for a non-commercial pharmacy model and should not be generalized or quoted as an industry statistic.

## 6. Implied inbound calls per location per month for small, medium and large specialty pharmacies

### Takeaway
Using URAC's derived 0.9 to 1.3 calls per specialty prescription and 8 to 12 fills per patient per year, a 500-patient pharmacy receives roughly 300 to 650 inbound business-hours calls a month (central about 460), a 2,000-patient pharmacy roughly 1,200 to 2,600 (central about 1,830), and a 10,000-patient pharmacy roughly 6,000 to 13,000 (central about 9,170). Confidence: low to medium. Roughly two-thirds of URAC-accredited organizations would fall inside the bands up to 3,000 calls a month.

### Cited Findings
- Inputs: calls and prescription totals from the three URAC reports (question 2); size tiers from URAC Figure 1 and ASHP (question 4); fills per patient from Vanderbilt (question 4); abandonment 2.98% from [URAC 2025 report](https://www.urac.org/sppasr25).

### Inferences

**Assumptions**
1. Calls per specialty prescription: low 0.9 (MY 2024), high 1.3 (MY 2022), central 1.1 (three-year average). Source: derived from URAC audited totals.
2. Fills per active patient per year: low 8, high 12, central 10 (Vanderbilt implies about 9.7; monthly dispensing caps near 12 to 13). The 8 to 12 range is my assumption, not a published statistic.
3. "Patients" means active patients on therapy, single location, one specialty drug each.
4. Business-hours inbound calls only, from all caller types. After-hours calls and outbound calls excluded.
5. 21 business days per month. Answered calls = inbound x (1 - 0.0298).

**Arithmetic: inbound calls per month = patients x (fills per year / 12) x calls per prescription**

| Size | Prescriptions per month (8 to 12 fills/yr) | Low (8 fills, 0.9) | Central (10 fills, 1.1) | High (12 fills, 1.3) | Central per business day | Central answered at 97% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small, 500 patients | 333 to 500 | 333 x 0.9 = 300 | 417 x 1.1 = 458 | 500 x 1.3 = 650 | about 22 | about 445 |
| Medium, 2,000 patients | 1,333 to 2,000 | 1,333 x 0.9 = 1,200 | 1,667 x 1.1 = 1,833 | 2,000 x 1.3 = 2,600 | about 87 | about 1,780 |
| Large, 10,000 patients | 6,667 to 10,000 | 6,667 x 0.9 = 6,000 | 8,333 x 1.1 = 9,167 | 10,000 x 1.3 = 13,000 | about 437 | about 8,890 |

Equivalent per-patient rate: 0.6 to 1.3 inbound calls per active patient per month, central about 0.9.

**Mapping to the pricing bands (central 1.1 calls per prescription, 10 fills per patient-year)**

| Band ceiling (answered calls/month) | Prescriptions per year | Approx. active patients |
|---|---|---|
| 750 | about 8,200 | about 820 |
| 1,500 | about 16,400 | about 1,640 |
| 2,250 | about 24,500 | about 2,450 |
| 3,000 | about 32,700 | about 3,270 |

- The 1,500-call ceiling lands almost exactly on URAC's 16,000-prescription tier boundary, so about 56% of URAC-accredited organizations sit in the first two bands. The 2,250-call ceiling is close to the 25,000-prescription boundary, so about 65% sit in the first three bands (56.13% + 9.25%). The 3,000-call band adds the bottom slice of the 25,000 to 99,999 tier (23.66% of organizations). Roughly 65 to 70% of URAC-accredited organizations would be priced on the published bands and 30 to 35% would be custom quotes. At the MY 2024 ratio of 0.91, the 3,000-call ceiling corresponds to about 39,600 prescriptions a year, which moves a little more of that tier into the bands.
- Health-system specialty pharmacies skew larger. On the ASHP 2024 distribution, 24% dispense 15,000 or fewer prescriptions (first two bands) and 44% dispense 30,000 or fewer; about 56% dispense more than 30,000 and would mostly exceed 3,000 calls a month. A health-system site at 60,001 to 75,000 prescriptions a year implies about 4,500 to 8,100 calls a month (0.9 to 1.3). ASHP respondents are 67% academic medical centers, so this overstates the size of the typical community-hospital specialty pharmacy.
- The band structure therefore fits independents and smaller provider-owned pharmacies well, and puts the majority of surveyed health-system specialty pharmacies and all PBM/chain sites into custom quote.
- Cross-check: the vendor-reported 12,000 calls a month for a non-commercial dispensing pharmacy operation is the size of the "large" scenario; it is not attributable to a single location.

**Confidence**
- Calls per prescription (0.9 to 1.3): medium. Audited accreditor totals, but volume-weighted toward the largest organizations, mixed specialty and non-specialty call traffic, and business hours only.
- Per-location scenarios: low to medium. They rest on an assumed fills-per-patient range and on applying an industry-aggregate ratio to small pharmacies, for which no stratified data exists. High-touch therapy mixes (oncology, rare disease, new starts needing prior authorization) could plausibly run well above the range; mature maintenance populations with texting and portals could run below it.

### Gaps
- No source reports inbound calls per specialty pharmacy location. Everything in this section is derived.
- No stratification of calls per prescription by pharmacy size or ownership, so the ratio for a 500-patient independent is an extrapolation from an aggregate dominated by national PBM-owned and chain pharmacies.
- After-hours inbound volume and outbound volume are not measured anywhere I found; a 24/7 agent would handle more than the business-hours figures above.
- Validation path: URAC-accredited prospects already produce monthly and quarterly telephone metric reports with total call counts for DTM2010-04, so actual volume can be requested directly during sales qualification.
